# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to making participation in Overdrive Archive welcoming,
respectful, and safe for everyone, regardless of background, identity,
experience, or technical ability.

This project handles unusually sensitive data, including vehicle recordings,
location history, access tokens, and home-network details. Respect for privacy
is therefore part of respectful community conduct, not only a security concern.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a healthy community include:

- being respectful and constructive;
- assuming good intent while discussing the technical facts;
- accepting feedback and correcting mistakes;
- keeping criticism focused on the work rather than the person;
- protecting private vehicle, location, credential, and infrastructure data;
- giving credit for ideas and contributions.

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, discrimination, threats, or personal attacks;
- sexualized language or unwanted attention;
- trolling, sustained disruption, or deliberate misinformation;
- doxxing or publishing another person's private information;
- sharing recordings, telemetry, locations, tokens, logs, or network details
  without the owner's informed permission;
- retaliating against someone who reports a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews,
community spaces, and private project communications. It also applies when
someone is publicly representing the project.

## Reporting

Do not include sensitive evidence in a public issue. Until a dedicated
moderation address is published, submit a
[private GitHub report](../../security/advisories/new) and begin the title with
`Code of Conduct:`. Include relevant links, dates, context, and the outcome you
are requesting. If private reporting is not enabled on the repository, open a
minimal public issue asking for a private contact channel without including the
sensitive evidence.

Maintainers will acknowledge reports as soon as practical, keep information
limited to people who need it, and avoid conflicts of interest. A report about
a maintainer should be handled by another maintainer whenever possible.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove content, request a correction, issue a warning, limit
participation temporarily, or permanently ban a participant. The response will
be proportional to the behavior, its impact, previous incidents, and whether
the person takes responsibility and repairs the harm.

Good-faith security research, respectful disagreement, and accidental mistakes
are not violations by themselves.
